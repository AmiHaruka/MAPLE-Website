ParmFit NCAA

Overview

NCAA is the parmfit(method=ncaa) route for a noncanonical amino acid whose peptide backbone is recognized but whose complete residue graph does not match an active standard Amber protein template. It builds capped ACE-target-NME models, where the backbone and caps are frozen during optimization so that only the sidechain R-group relaxes. The α and β conformers are built by rotating the backbone to target (φ,ψ) values of approximately (−60°, −40°) and (−120°, 140°) for an L residue; the signs are reversed for a D residue. ncaa_resids accepts several comma-separated residues; each one is built in its own work directory and all are deployed together. After charge fitting (see ChargeFit), AmberTools prepin/frcmod templates are created, and bond/angle terms plus sidechain torsions are refined. Deployment is validated with tleap. Standard residues may also be declared deliberately when they should be rebuilt through this flow.

For a worked walkthrough, see the NCAA tutorial.

Target

ncaa_resids must identify protein residues in the input PDB, one selector per comma-separated entry: a chain and residue number such as A123, a residue name and number such as HIF123, or an appended altloc label when a specific alternate conformation is required, for example A123A for altloc A. A selector without a chain must still be unique. Declare multiple residues by comma-separating their selectors, as in ncaa_resids=HIF89,TRP482. Residue selection occurs after standard-residue graph matching and does not control that recognition. Every selected residue must have a connected N-H-CA-C(=O) peptide backbone and at least one sidechain heavy atom bonded to CA; ncaa_resnames controls only its output residue name.

The charges in ncaa_charges apply only to the declared residues. Other backbone-only residues in the same PDB do not inherit their charge or generated NCAA template and remain explicitly reported for separate handling.

Workflow

The formal statement of the route:

  1. Target identity. Locate the target residue and detect backbone, sidechain anchor, and chirality.
  2. Capped models. Build and optimize α and β ACE-target-NME conformers.
  3. Atomic charges. Fit charges on the optimized conformers with the configured chg_fit route and prepare the charged capped mol2.
  4. AmberTools template. Use the fitted charges with antechamber, prepgen, and parmchk2 to create the initial prepin/frcmod files.
  5. Parameter refinement. Apply Seminario/mSeminario to bond/angle terms and TorsionFit to target-residue sidechain torsions.
  6. Deployment. Write refined prepin/frcmod, processed protein PDB, tleap input, and tleap validation outputs.

Preserving original GAFF2 parameters

Bond/angle and torsion refinement are controlled separately. Setting bonded=none keeps the initial BOND/ANGLE terms; setting torsionfit=false keeps the initial DIHE terms. When either refinement is enabled, atom types are automatically reassigned (MAPLE Z0/Z1, etc.).

ChargeFit

NCAA fits charges on the optimized α and β capped conformers. With chg_fit=resp, Gaussian evaluates the ESP of both conformers and AmberTools performs one joint two-stage RESP fit. A charge-capable MLIP or Antechamber method evaluates the two conformers separately, after which MAPLE applies their equal-weight per-atom average. For model and Antechamber routes, a final backbone adjustment closes the target-residue charge in the prepin to the ncaa_charges value.

chg_fit is independent of iqm. RESP derivation always runs Gaussian for the ESP and AmberTools for the RESP stages, controlled by chg_level, chg_route, qm_nproc, and qm_mem. NCAA cannot use chg_fit=none because a PDB input does not supply a reusable set of atomic charges.

All charge-route files remain under <base>_work/ncaa/<tag>/, one directory per declared residue. The resulting charged capped mol2 and target.chg feed the prepin/GAFF2 template build; the same charges are carried into the Stage 0 parameter set (the bonded-refined starting point) and the TorsionFit baseline.

TorsionFit

NCAA restricts torsion fitting to rotatable dihedrals in the target residue's sidechain; backbone–backbone and cap dihedrals are excluded. torsion_bonds uses residue-local 1-based indices (ACE/NME caps not counted) and separates per-residue groups with ;, in the same order as ncaa_resids. For residues with particularly complex sidechains, consider setting torsionfit=false and relying on the original GAFF2 torsion and parmchk2 improper parameters, or use torsion_bonds to refine specific dihedrals selectively. NCAA disables torsion ensemble targets internally; see the main ParmFit page for scan, improper-refit, and refinement parameter details.

Peptide Boundary Terms

When the NCAA residue is inserted as a custom template, MAPLE dynamically generates ProteinFF-GAFF2 cross-terms at both peptide boundaries: the head side (prev_C–target_N) and the tail side (target_C–next_N). These include exact BOND, ANGLE, and DIHE terms that connect the custom template atom types to the neighbouring standard protein residue atom types.

Input Files

Simple Input

A simple NCAA job declares method=ncaa inline and keeps its commonly used ParmFit options in a sidecar config. The declared residues select the noncanonical protein residues, and the PDB block supplies the complete structure.

#model=uma(size=uma-s-1p1)
#parmfit(method=ncaa,input=NCAA.in)
#device=gpu0

PDB ncaa.pdb
Example simple ParmFit config 
ncaa_resids=A123
ncaa_resnames=MOL
ncaa_charges=0
ncaa_mults=1
chg_fit=resp
bonded=mseminario
torsionfit=false

Full External Input

For a longer setup, keep the MAPLE launcher short and move ParmFit parameters into an external .parmfit file. Declare method=ncaa inline together with input=; the external config must not set method=. The PDB remains in the MAPLE input; do not place pdb, input, or global MAPLE options in the external config.

#model=uma(size=uma-s-1p1)
#parmfit(method=ncaa,input=NCAA.parmfit)
#device=gpu0

PDB ncaa.pdb

Full ParmFit config

NCAA.parmfit 
# QM reference
iqm=false
qm_engine=g16
opt_level=B3LYP/def2-SVP
sp_level=B3LYP/def2-SVP
opt_route=
sp_route=
qm_mode=1
qm_nproc=8
qm_mem=24

# Atomic charges
chg_fit=resp
resp_backend=gaussian
chg_level=HF/6-31G(d)
chg_route=

# Residue declaration, force field, and bonded fitting
ncaa_resids=A123
ncaa_charges=0
ncaa_mults=1
ncaa_resnames=MOL
pro_ff=ff14SB
wat_ff=tip3p
bonded=mseminario
vib_scale=1.0

# Choose the altloc conformer for these residues (selector + letter, e.g. PHE393B)
keep=

# NCAA capped-conformer optimization controls
opt_max_iter=256
opt_max_step=0.2

# TorsionFit
torsionfit=false
torsion_bonds=
p_thresh=30.0
radical_center=
torsion_steps=36
backend=cgbs
constraint_mode=projected

torsion_refine_rounds=3
torsion_refine_max_iter=256
torsion_refine_tol=1.0e-6
torsion_ensemble=false
torsion_ensemble_ratio=0.3
torsion_ensemble_weight=0.5

Output Files

  • <base>_work/<rn>.prepin and <base>_work/<rn>.frcmod: Initial AmberTools residue template files.
  • <base>_work/<rn>_maple.prepin and <base>_work/<rn>_maple.frcmod: Refined MAPLE residue template and parameter files.
  • <base>_work/<base>_ncaa_tleap.pdb and <base>_work/<base>_ncaa_tleap.in: Processed protein PDB and tleap input.
  • <base>_work/<base>_ncaa.prmtop, <base>_work/<base>_ncaa.inpcrd, and solvated PDB outputs: tleap validation products.
  • <base>_work/ncaa/<RESNAME><RESSEQ>/: Per-residue optimized conformers, charge-route files, charged capped mol2, target.chg, AmberTools template intermediates, and torsion-refinement data.

Requirements and Limits

  • The declared residues must have a connected peptide backbone and enough sidechain identity for capping and chirality detection; standard atom naming is not required.
  • chg_fit=none is unsupported because NCAA has no input mol2 charges to preserve.
  • When selected, RESP uses the Gaussian backend; model and Antechamber charge routes do not require RESP.
  • The final target-residue charge written for model and Antechamber methods is constrained to the ncaa_charges value.
  • TorsionFit is restricted to rotatable dihedrals in the target residue's sidechain and does not fit peptide backbone torsions.
